Six things in that call move the plan. One of them changes what you are building, and one changes how you will be measured.

Present John, Deanna, Stuart, Tom Hagerman Absent Stu Maggs, Adam Next back half of next week

00The two that matter most

They rejected a standalone product. John, Tom and Deanna all said it independently: nobody learns a new system. What ships is an OmniShift tab on the Salesforce record, plus alerts and a dashboard. John: "This is like the engine behind that they don't even necessarily need to see." Your prototype is now the design spec for the engine, not the delivery surface.

The number they quoted is not the number you move. John gave you 34% — initial meeting held to agreement signed. That conversion happens after a BD advisor is already in a four-meeting sales process. The agent operates upstream of that. If 34% becomes the success metric, you will be judged on something you barely touch.

01What changed

CHANGE 01

The delivery surface is Salesforce, and Salesforce is a tangled web

Deanna was blunt: "even for us to make small integration changes, I have to hire a consultant for 100 hours… the infrastructure in Salesforce is a tangled web." That moves Salesforce integration from a late-stage detail onto the critical path, and it makes Stu Maggs the most important person you have not yet met.

The hardest consequence is not display — it is feedback capture. Your learning loop depends on recording what the CA did with each recommendation. In a standalone app that is a button. Inside Salesforce it is custom fields, page layout changes and adoption discipline. Design for it now.

DoGet Stu Maggs on the next call. Ask what a new tab, a custom object and a dashboard component actually cost in his environment before you promise any of it.
CHANGE 02

A second product appeared: lead-to-advisor matching

John led with it and it is not in any scope you have written. He wants the agent to route each lead to the person most likely to convert it: "Gus is really good dealing with this type of person… Claire is the best at closing this type of executive." Deanna offered to build the input — a database of the BD and CA teams with certifications, strengths and geography.

This is a genuinely different model from prospect ranking. It needs per-person historical conversion broken down by segment, and TMG almost certainly has not assembled that. Tom's current routing is geography plus a lead-owner field.

DoScope it separately and say so. It is a strong second phase, and it will stall Phase 1 if it gets bundled in.
CHANGE 03

The bottleneck has a name, and it may not be yours to fix

Tom named it twice, unprompted: there is no suppression between WealthFeed and Salesforce. Every CA manually cross-references each prospect to check they are not already in the database. Marketing hits the same wall on ABM campaigns. Deanna hits it again in VisitIQ — "clients are being suppressed out of here, we're still figuring that out."

This is your Phase 0, now validated by the user rather than hypothesised. But note the awkward part: suppression is arguably WealthFeed's job, and Deanna has the CEO's mobile. Raise it with them first. Winning by making the client's existing vendor fix their own gap buys more trust than building around it.

DoPut suppression at the top of the WealthFeed vendor call. If they will not ship it, you build it and it is a fast, provable win.
CHANGE 04

WealthFeed is narrower than feared — the overlap risk is largely gone

The question you most needed answered got answered. WealthFeed is used for identification only: search by ICP criteria, wealth data, and "catalysts" (inheritance, job change, relocation). Rolled out firm-wide at the start of July after an eight-week pilot. Tom reports immediate impact on client size and pipeline.

No outreach automation, no sequencer, no Proofpoint compliance flow in use. CAs call and email by hand. That leaves the entire decision layer, the messaging layer and the supervision layer open — which is the scope you wanted.

DoStop treating WealthFeed as a competitor. It is an upstream data source. Say that plainly next time; it will land well.
CHANGE 05

Tom handed you the narrowest valuable first feature

"If I make a phone call and an AI agent, compliance approved, sends an email following my phone call, that's something that would definitely create some more efficiency."

That is small, unambiguous, sits exactly on your existing compliance capability, and the end user asked for it himself. It also fits the day-1 cadence he described — call, voicemail, email, all on the same day — and the copy-paste burden he called a bottleneck.

DoConsider making this the visible Phase 1 alongside suppression. Adoption is guaranteed when the user specified the feature.
CHANGE 06

The best dataset in the building is untouched

Every prospect and client meeting is recorded in Zoom Revenue Accelerator. Gevorg spotted it and nobody followed up. With a 34% close rate over four meetings and cycles from three weeks to years, those recordings hold the actual reasons deals convert — the thing no CRM field captures.

DoAdd it to the data request. Ask what Revenue Accelerator exposes via API, and whether transcripts can be exported.

04Vendor questions — ready to send

Deanna is routing these to the CEOs directly: Sam Kendree at WealthFeed, and "Vern" at VisitIQ. She noted TMG got in early with both and is helping shape the platforms — so ask for what you need, not only for what exists.

WealthFeed for Sam Kendree

Access and integration

  1. Do you expose a REST API for programmatic access to prospect records and wealth events? If so, what authentication, and is there developer documentation?
  2. What are the rate limits and volume ceilings per account?
  3. Can we receive events by webhook or push when a catalyst fires, rather than polling?
  4. How does the existing Salesforce integration work — native managed package, middleware, or export? Which objects and fields does it write?
  5. Is there a bulk or batch endpoint for enriching a list of existing records, as opposed to one lookup at a time?

The suppression gap — lead with this

  1. TMG's largest operational bottleneck is that WealthFeed does not suppress records that already exist in their Salesforce. Client associates cross-reference by hand on every prospect. Is bidirectional suppression on your roadmap, and on what timeline?
  2. If not, can we push a suppression list to you via API and have it respected in search results and exports?
  3. What identity resolution key do you match on — email, name plus address, a proprietary person ID? Is that ID stable and can it be stored in Salesforce for future matching?

Data, provenance and licensing

  1. For each field — net worth, investable assets, income, age — is the value observed or modelled, and do you expose a confidence score?
  2. What are the 16 catalyst types, what is the detection latency for each, and how is recency stamped?
  3. What does the licence permit regarding derived works — may we compute and store scores derived from your data inside TMG's environment?
  4. Any restriction on passing your data to a third-party processor such as ourselves, and do you need to approve us as a sub-processor?
  5. What is the source and refresh cadence of the underlying data, and how are opt-outs and do-not-contact requests handled?

VisitIQ for Vern

Access and integration

  1. Is there a REST API for identified visitor records and their journeys? Authentication, rate limits, documentation?
  2. Can identifications be delivered by webhook in near real time, so an alert can reach a client associate the same day?
  3. How does the Salesforce push work today — which objects, which fields, and is it configurable?
  4. Is the journey data (page sequence, UTM source, timestamps) available through the API, or only in the interface? Deanna noted it does not appear on every record — why?

Match quality — ask directly

  1. What is your measured accuracy for person-level identification, and how was it measured? Would you support a known-visitor test where TMG sends five known people to the site and we check what comes back?
  2. Do you return a confidence score per identification, and can it be filtered on?
  3. TMG's uploaded list produced records with implausible net worth values. What causes that, and is there a way to flag low-confidence enrichments rather than returning them silently?
  4. Can we push a client and do-not-contact suppression list so existing clients never surface? Deanna is still working around this manually.

Consent, provenance and compliance

  1. What exactly is the consent basis for identification, and what does the pixel capture before consent is given?
  2. Do you provide an auditable record of consent per identified visitor — timestamp, banner version, choice made? TMG is an SEC-registered adviser and needs to evidence this.
  3. How do you handle state privacy law opt-outs and deletion requests, and what is the turnaround?
  4. Which fields are observed versus modelled, and what are the licence terms on derived works and third-party processing?
One line to include in the covering email

"Answers to the API and identity-key questions are what let us design the integration properly — if we get those back before the next session, we can bring an architecture rather than a set of options."
